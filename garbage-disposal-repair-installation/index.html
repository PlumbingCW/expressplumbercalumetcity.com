When a disposal problem becomes an emergency

A disposal that leaks under the sink, a sink full of dirty water or a unit that has tripped the breaker interrupts the whole kitchen. Our plumbers answer 24/7 and can often come the same day. Turn the switch off, flip the breaker and keep your hand out of the unit.

If the sink is backing up, stop running water and do not run the disposal. A jam is one cause, but a clog in the drain beyond the disposal is also common, and our drain cleaning team can clear the line.

What is wrong with the disposal?

Humming but not turning

The blades are jammed. A plumber can free them with the hex socket at the bottom or by removing the jam.

Leak from the bottom

A cracked housing means the unit has failed and should be replaced.

Leak at the sink flange or drain

A loose or worn gasket and mounting. These are repairable.

Nothing happens at all

A tripped reset button or breaker, or a failed motor.

Bad smell

Food debris and grease in the grinding chamber. A cleaning and drain check help.

What should and should not go into a disposal

Treat the disposal gently

  • ✓Run cold water before, during and after use
  • ✓Grind small food scraps, not large amounts at once
  • ✓Keep grease, oil and fat out of the sink
  • ✓Avoid fibrous items like celery, corn husks and potato peels
  • ✓Keep bones, coffee grounds in bulk and eggshells in large amounts out

Kitchens, grease and the drain beyond the disposal

Grease in a kitchen line causes repeated backups in homes and in businesses on commercial corridors such as 159th Street and Sibley Boulevard, even when the disposal itself is fine. A line that clogs after the disposal is a drain problem, not a disposal problem.

If your kitchen line backs up repeatedly, a camera or jetting visit finds the cause. Our hydro jetting page explains what that involves.
