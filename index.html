What counts as a plumbing emergency

Call an emergency plumber when water is escaping and you cannot stop it, when sewage is coming up a drain, when a gas water heater smells wrong or when you have no heat or hot water in winter. Those are the calls our 24/7 line is built for, and a plumber will help you work out what to do first and whether it can wait until morning.

A dripping faucet, a slow drain or a toilet that runs can usually wait for a daytime appointment. If you are not sure, call anyway. A short conversation costs nothing and often settles it.

Water you cannot stop

A burst pipe, a failed supply line or a valve that will not close. Shut off the main and call. See burst pipe repair.

Sewage in the house

A tub, shower or floor drain that fills or smells. Stop using water and call. See sewer line cleaning.

No hot water

A leaking or dead water heater is an emergency in January. See water heater repair.

A flooding basement

A silent sump pump in a storm needs a plumber fast. See sump pump repair.

Plumbing in Calumet City: lake water, floods and older pipes

Calumet City sits in Cook County along the Illinois and Indiana state line, a few miles from the Little Calumet River. According to the Calumet City Water Department's 2024 report, the city draws all of its drinking water from Lake Michigan, with 20 percent supplied by Chicago and 80 percent by Hammond, Indiana. That tells you where your water comes from, and it is the first fact to bring to any conversation about filters or softeners.

The city's flood prevention page lists six major floods on the Little Calumet since 1981, and a levee built after the floods of 1981 and 1982. Heavy rain also tests the sewers. If your floor drain backs up in a storm or your basement takes on water, mention the weather when you call, because it changes where we look first.

The city's 2024 report also lists a lead 90th percentile of 17 parts per billion against a 15 ppb action level and explains that lead in drinking water comes primarily from service lines and home plumbing. It points residents to the lead service line inventory on calumetcity.org. When we open a wall or a trench, we can tell you what material is in it.
