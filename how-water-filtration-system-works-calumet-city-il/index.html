How a Water Filtration System Works: A Calumet City, IL Guide

Filters do not all do the same thing. This guide explains how the main types work and how to choose one based on a test, not a sales pitch.

The basic idea behind a filter

A water filter forces water through a material that traps or chemically binds unwanted substances. Different materials catch different things. A sediment filter catches particles. Activated carbon catches chlorine and some organic compounds. Reverse osmosis pushes water through a membrane that blocks many dissolved substances.

No single filter removes everything. The right choice depends on what is in your water and what you want to fix. We start with a test, and our water softener and filtration page explains how we recommend systems.

What a softener does and does not do

A softener is not a filter in the usual sense. It exchanges hardness minerals for sodium or potassium so scale does not build up in pipes and heaters. It does not remove chlorine, lead or most contaminants. A person on a sodium-restricted diet should ask a doctor about the sodium added by a softener.

Where filters go

Point of use

An under-sink or faucet filter treats drinking and cooking water at one tap.

Whole house

A system on the main line treats all the water in the house, often a sediment filter plus carbon.

At the water heater

A softener or scale filter protects the heater and the pipes after it.

Refrigerator and ice maker

A small inline filter often at the supply valve.

Filter media wear out. A carbon filter that is overdue can release what it trapped and grow bacteria. Mark replacement dates on a calendar and keep spare cartridges.

Calumet City local note: what the 2024 report lists

The Calumet City Water Department's 2024 report says the city draws 100 percent of its drinking water from Lake Michigan, with 20 percent supplied by Chicago and 80 percent by Hammond, and that the water met all federal and state standards. It notes that mandatory treatment includes coagulation, sedimentation, filtration and disinfection, and that fluoride is added.

The report also lists results from monitoring for 29 PFAS compounds and lithium under the federal UCMR5 program, and shows two PFAS compounds detected at an entry point in September 2024 at about three parts per trillion. No health-based standards had been set for them under the Safe Drinking Water Act.

The report lists a lead 90th percentile of 17 parts per billion against a 15 ppb action level. We cannot interpret health effects. Ask your doctor and the city water department, and choose a filter certified for the contaminant you care about.

What to do before you buy a system

A short checklist

  • ✓Read the latest city water quality report and write down the contaminant you actually care about
  • ✓Look for a filter certified by an accredited body for that specific contaminant
  • ✓Ask for the replacement cartridge price and schedule before you buy
  • ✓Do not use a filter on water that is unsafe to drink unless the label says it is meant for that
  • ✓Call your doctor, not a plumber, about any health question

How to choose without guessing

1

Read the city report

It lists what was found at the entry points and what the city monitors.

2

Test your tap

A test strip shows hardness and chlorine. A lab test checks lead, which depends on your own plumbing.

3

Match the tool to the problem

Scale needs a softener. Taste needs carbon. Lead needs a certified filter and often pipe replacement.

4

Maintain it

Replace cartridges and service the unit on the schedule in the manual.

If your house has lead service line concerns, a filter is a stopgap and not a repair. Our repiping page explains pipe replacement.

Water for Calumet City and for Lansing comes from the same Lake Michigan supply through Hammond, so the testing advice here applies in both. Our service areas page covers where we install systems.
