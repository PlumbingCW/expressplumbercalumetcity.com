Galvanized Pipe Plumbing: Calumet City, IL Homeowner Guide

Galvanized steel supply pipe was common in houses built many decades ago. It corrodes from the inside, and that quietly changes how your plumbing behaves.

What galvanized pipe is and how to spot it

Galvanized pipe is steel pipe coated with zinc to slow rust, and it was widely used for water supply lines in houses built before copper and plastic took over. If your house is old and has gray metal pipes joined with threaded fittings, it may be galvanized. A magnet will stick to it, which separates it from copper and from lead.

The zinc coating wears away over decades, and the steel underneath rusts. Rust builds up inside the pipe, so the opening narrows and water pressure falls. If you want the practical decision about replacing it, our repiping page covers the process.

Signs galvanized pipe is failing

Low pressure that keeps getting worse

The inside of the pipe is closing up. One fixture may be fine and the far bathroom a trickle.

Brown or orange water

Rust flakes loosen when water has sat in the pipe, so the first draw of the morning looks dirty.

Leaks at threads

Corrosion concentrates at the threaded joints, and a pinhole leak at a fitting is a common first symptom.

Pipe that feels rough or gritty inside

A plumber cutting into the line often finds a wall of rust scale where the pipe should be smooth.

A whole-house repipe is a bigger project, but it often costs less than years of repairs and it solves the pressure problem. Modern PEX or copper lines last for decades and do not rust. A plumber can plan the work in phases so you can keep using the house.

Why galvanized pipe and old houses go together

A house built before the 1960s was often plumbed in galvanized steel, with cast iron or clay drains. A 2025 water affordability plan for the Village of Lansing, published through the University of Illinois Chicago, reports that 39 percent of Lansing homes were built in 1959 or earlier. Calumet City's own housing age is not given in the sources we reviewed, so we do not claim a figure, but nearby older housing is a reason to look at your pipes.

Calumet City local note: lead, service lines and the city inventory

The Calumet City Water Department's 2024 report says lead in drinking water comes primarily from service lines and home plumbing, and it lists a lead 90th percentile of 17 parts per billion against a 15 ppb action level. It advises flushing taps for several minutes before drinking and using a filter certified by an accredited body if you are concerned.

The report also says the city's lead service line inventory is available at calumetcity.org. Galvanized pipe downstream of a lead service line can trap lead particles, so a repipe is a good time to ask about the line from the street. Our water service line repair page explains how that work goes.

How a replacement works

1

Survey

The plumber traces the lines and finds where to open walls.

2

Plan phases

Work goes room by room so water is off only for short periods.

3

Install new lines

New PEX or copper lines replace the old, and fixtures reconnect.

4

Test and patch

Every line is pressure tested and the access holes are patched.

Expect some drywall repair. A good plumber chooses routes that use closets, basement ceilings and attic spaces to avoid opening finished walls. Ask for a written scope that says who patches and paints.

What affects the cost

House size, number of bathrooms, wall access, the pipe material you choose and local permit rules drive the price. Replacing a single branch costs a fraction of a whole-house project. Permit rules vary, so ask the Calumet City Building and Zoning Department what applies to your address.

If you live in Calumet City or in Lansing, where older housing is common, a plumber can look at your pipes. Our service areas page lists the nearby towns we serve.

Replacement piping in Illinois must meet the Illinois Plumbing Code, and the work must be done by a licensed plumber under the Illinois Plumbing License Law. Ask which pipe material the plumber plans to use and why.

Questions about galvanized pipe in Calumet City

Look at the pipe where it enters the house near the meter. Lead is dull gray, soft and a magnet will not stick. The city's 2024 report says its lead service line inventory is posted on calumetcity.org, and a plumber can confirm what you have.

If it has no leaks and pressure is fine, you can keep it for now. It will keep corroding, so plan for replacement.

Rust is mostly a nuisance, but old galvanized pipe can also carry lead from upstream. If you are concerned, test your water and ask the city.

A small house may take a few days. Larger houses take longer.
