What Causes Plumbing Leaks? A Calumet City, IL Homeowner Guide

Pipes leak for a handful of predictable reasons. Knowing them helps you catch a small problem before it turns into a ruined ceiling.

The main causes of plumbing leaks

Most plumbing leaks come from corrosion, high water pressure, worn seals and fittings, freezing, shifting ground and physical damage. A leak is rarely random. The pipe or joint has been under stress for a long time, and the leak is where the weakest point finally gave way.

Knowing the cause matters because it tells you whether a repair will last. If you are already seeing water, our leak detection team can find the source. If you want to understand why it happened, read on.

Corrosion: the slow one

Water slowly attacks metal. Galvanized steel rusts from the inside out, narrows and eventually leaks at threads. Copper can develop pinholes when the water chemistry, flow or acidity is wrong. Corrosion tends to show up in several places over time, which is why a single repair on an old line often leads to another one.

If you have leaked more than once in the same kind of pipe, talk to a plumber about repiping the affected run, instead of paying for a series of patches.

Pressure, seals and everyday wear

High pressure

Pressure above about 80 psi stresses fixtures. A gauge on a hose bib tells you what you have, and a pressure regulator fixes it.

Old supply lines

Braided lines and rubber washing machine hoses fail with age. Replace them every several years.

Water heater parts

Relief valves, drain valves and tank fittings leak before the tank itself does.

Toilet and faucet parts

A worn flapper or cartridge wastes water for months before anyone notices.

Hidden leaks and how to catch them

Check the water meter with every fixture off. If the dial moves, water is leaving the system. Look under sinks, behind toilets and around the water heater once a month. Watch for stains, bubbled paint, a musty smell or a rise in your water bill. Our water meter and high-bill page explains the test step by step.

Calumet City local note: what the city says about pipes and lead

The Calumet City Water Department's 2024 report says lead and copper in drinking water come from the corrosion of household plumbing systems and the erosion of natural deposits, and it lists those as the likely source in its lead and copper table. It also says lead comes primarily from service lines and home plumbing, which the city cannot control inside your house.

That is a useful reminder that corrosion is not just a leak risk. If your home has older pipes, the report advises flushing taps before drinking and using a certified filter if you are concerned, and it points to the city's lead service line inventory on calumetcity.org. A plumber who opens a wall can tell you what the pipe is made of.

What to do when you find a leak

1

Stop the water

Close the fixture valve or the main, depending on the source.

2

Limit the damage

Move belongings, mop up and run a fan. Photograph everything.

3

Find the source

Look above the stain, along the pipe and at the nearest fixture.

4

Call a plumber

A plumber confirms the cause and repairs it, and can tell you if the rest of the line is fragile.
