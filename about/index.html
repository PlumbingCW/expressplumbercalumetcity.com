How to check any plumber's license in Illinois

The Illinois Department of Public Health says that people who plan, install, alter, repair and maintain plumbing in Illinois must be licensed under the Illinois Plumbing License Law, and that plumbing contractors must also register with the state. IDPH offers a Plumbing License Verification tool on its website. We encourage you to use it for any plumber you hire, including us.

State law also requires that a plumbing business display at least one licensed plumber's license at its place of business. If you want to see what licensing your plumber holds, ask when you call. For permits on bigger jobs, ask the Calumet City Building and Zoning Department what applies to your address.

Why we answer by phone only

Plumbing problems are urgent and hard to describe in a form field. A homeowner standing in an inch of water wants to talk to a person who can say what to do in the next five minutes. A phone call also lets us ask the one question that matters, such as whether the water is still running, and that question rarely fits a drop-down menu.

Keeping everything on one line also keeps our promises simple. The number on this page is the number that rings, whether it is noon on a Tuesday or three in the morning on a holiday weekend.

Plumbing problems we see in Calumet City

Calumet City sits near the Little Calumet River and the Indiana state line, and many homes in the area were built decades ago. Older homes often have older drain lines and water heaters, which is why drain clogs, water heater failures and sump pump trouble are common calls. If your basement has taken on water in past storms, a working sump pump and a clear floor drain matter more than almost any other plumbing fixture in the house.

For slow leaks you cannot find, our leak detection service helps locate the problem without opening walls blindly. For a sudden break, the burst pipe repair page explains what to do in the first few minutes.
