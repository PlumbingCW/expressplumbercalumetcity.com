How to Install a Toilet Tank: What Calumet City, IL Homes Need to Know

Replacing a toilet tank is a manageable weekend job if the bowl is sound. This guide walks through the steps, the parts and the mistakes that cause leaks.

Quick answer: can you replace just the tank?

Yes, on most two-piece toilets you can replace the tank without replacing the bowl, as long as the new tank matches the bowl's brand, model and bolt spacing. Mismatched parts leak. If the bowl is cracked, wobbly or very old, replacing the whole toilet is the smarter move.

The job takes an hour or two. You shut off the water, drain the tank, unbolt it from the bowl, swap the gasket and install the new tank with fresh bolts and a fill valve. If anything feels stuck or the supply line is corroded, our toilet repair and installation team can finish it.

What you need before you start

Gather these first

  • ✓The new tank that matches your bowl's model, found on the inside of the old tank or under the lid
  • ✓A tank-to-bowl gasket and bolt kit, even if the tank comes with one
  • ✓A new fill valve and flapper if the tank does not include them
  • ✓A new braided supply line, because old ones fail without warning
  • ✓A bucket, towels, a sponge, an adjustable wrench and a screwdriver

Step by step

1

Shut off and drain

Close the valve behind the toilet, flush and hold the handle until the tank empties. Sponge out what is left.

2

Disconnect

Unscrew the supply line from the tank. Remove the two bolts under the tank that hold it to the bowl.

3

Lift off the old tank

Lift straight up and set it aside. Remove the old gasket and clean the bowl's rim.

4

Install the new tank

Set the new gasket, lower the tank, seat it level and tighten the bolts evenly, alternating sides, to a snug fit. Do not overtighten porcelain.

Reconnect the supply line, open the valve slowly and let the tank fill. Check every joint with a dry paper towel and flush several times. A drip at the bolts means the washers or gasket are not seated. Tighten a quarter turn at a time.

Calumet City local note: licensing, permits and who can do the work

The Illinois Department of Public Health says that people who install, alter and repair plumbing in Illinois must be licensed under the Illinois Plumbing License Law. Whether a homeowner swapping a tank on their own toilet needs anything extra can depend on the municipality.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Calumet City Building and Zoning Department before you start, and ask your village if you live in a nearby community such as Lansing. If you hire a plumber, the state offers an online license verification tool, and we welcome you to use it for any contractor.

When to call a plumber instead

Call if the shutoff valve will not close, if the supply line is corroded to the valve, if the floor around the toilet is soft or stained or if you see sewage. Those are signs of a bigger problem. Tools and parts to have on hand

Gather a screwdriver, an adjustable wrench, a sponge, a bucket, rubber gloves and a new tank-to-bowl gasket and bolt set. Check the model number on the tank and the bowl before you buy a tank, because tanks are matched to bowls. A mismatched tank may fit but leak.

How to test for leaks afterward

Fill the tank and wait ten minutes. Dry the outside of the tank and the base of the bowl with a paper towel, then look for beads of water at the bolts and at the supply connection. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and watch the bowl. If the color appears without a flush, the flapper is leaking.

Tighten bolts a little at a time and alternate sides. Overtightening can crack the porcelain.
