What a tankless water heater does

A tankless water heater heats water only when a tap is open. Cold water enters the unit, a gas burner or electric element heats it, and the heated water leaves. There is no tank to rust or leak, so there is no standby heat loss and no running out in the middle of a shower.

The catch is capacity. A unit can only heat so many gallons per minute. If you run a shower and a dishwasher at the same time, the unit must be sized for both. We calculate this before we recommend a model, so you know what to expect.

Is tankless right for your house?

Gas line capacity

Tankless gas units draw more gas at once than a tank. A small gas line may need an upgrade.

Venting

Many tankless units need special venting. Reusing an old chimney flue often does not work.

Household use

A large family with simultaneous showers needs a larger unit or two.

Space and budget

A wall-hung unit frees floor space. The upfront cost is higher than a tank.

Tankless service: flush, descale and repair

A tankless unit that is never serviced loses efficiency as scale builds on the heat exchanger. Symptoms include lukewarm water, loud boiling or popping sounds and error codes. Flushing the unit with a descaling solution and a small pump clears the scale. Many manufacturers recommend an annual flush, and the interval depends on your water.

If a unit is past its useful life or the cost of a repair is high, we can discuss replacement with either another tankless unit or a tank. For a conventional heater problem, our water heater repair page may be a better fit.

Lake Michigan water and tankless scale

Calumet City's 2024 water quality report says the city's water comes entirely from Lake Michigan. The report we reviewed does not publish a hardness figure, so we do not guess one. Hardness varies, and how fast scale builds depends on it.

A simple test strip from a hardware store or a call to the Calumet City water department can tell you your hardness. If it is high, a softener or scale inhibitor protects a tankless unit. Our water softener and filtration page explains the options.
