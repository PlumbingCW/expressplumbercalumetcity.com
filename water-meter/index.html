Start with a five-minute meter test

Turn off every faucet, the washer, the dishwasher and the ice maker. Find your water meter, usually in the basement near where the line enters, or in a pit outside. Look for a small triangle or gear on the dial that spins when water moves. If it keeps turning with everything off, water is leaving your system.

Write down the reading, wait 30 minutes without using water, and read it again. If the number has changed, you have a leak. If it has not, the extra usage may be something that happens only when a fixture runs, such as a toilet that sticks open.

A running toilet is the most common cause

A toilet with a worn flapper can waste hundreds of gallons a day without making much sound. It is often the cheapest fix. We replace the flapper and fill valve, and adjust the chain. If the toilet is old and has cracked porcelain or constant problems, our toilet repair and installation page explains when to replace.

When the meter says the leak is underground

If the meter moves with every fixture closed and your toilets pass the test, the leak is likely in the supply line. That could be a pipe under a slab, a line inside a wall or the service line between the meter and house. Our leak detection team uses sound sensors and pressure tests to pinpoint it. If it turns out to be the pipe from the street, see water service line repair.

Calumet City water bills and the meter

The city's water department reads and bills for water in Calumet City, and the 2024 water quality report lists the department as the contact for questions. If a bill is much higher than usual, call the department to ask when the meter was last read and whether the bill was estimated, and bring that information to us.

A leak that began after the last reading may only show in a later bill. If you can see a dial or a digital register, take a photo today, and again tomorrow. Two photos 24 hours apart document whether water is moving when no one is home.
