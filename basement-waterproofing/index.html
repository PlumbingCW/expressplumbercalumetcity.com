Where basement water comes from

Basement water has several sources, and the fix depends on which one it is. Groundwater pushes up through the floor and cove joint. Surface water runs along the foundation after rain. Sewer or drain water backs up through floor drains. A leaking supply line adds its own moisture.

A plumber looks for the source first. If the water is clear and appears along walls after rain, drainage and grading are likely. If the water smells or is gray, it is sewer water and you need backflow protection. If it appears when no rain has fallen, a pipe may be leaking, and our leak detection team can check.

Why we start with the sump and the drain

Waterproofing starts with a plan for the water you collect. A drain tile system without a working sump pump just moves the problem. We check the pit, the pump, the discharge and the power, and we discuss a battery backup. See our sump pump page for details.

For sewer backup, a drain system cannot help. A backwater valve or overhead sewer addresses it, and the city's flood page recommends these for repeat-backup homes. We tell you which problem you have.

What Calumet City residents face

The city of Calumet City's flood prevention page lists the November 1990 flood as the highest recorded on the Little Calumet River and notes a levee built after the 1981 and 1982 floods. In July 2023, local reports said Governor Pritzker declared Calumet City a disaster area after heavy rain, and officials said much of the damage came from sewer backups.

Reports at the time also described a city aid program covering about half of the cost of home sewer improvements, and the city says residents can buy flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program. Programs change, so ask the city what is available today before you start work.
