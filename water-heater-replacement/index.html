Emergency water heater replacement: what happens on the call

A failed tank, a flooded utility room or a house with no hot water in January is a replacement emergency. We take these calls 24/7 and often replace the heater the same day, including evenings and weekends. Turn off the cold water supply to the tank, switch off the gas control or the breaker, and leave the room clear for us.

When you call, tell us the make and capacity printed on the data plate, whether the heater is gas or electric, and where it sits. If the tank is leaking from the body, we will plan for replacement before we arrive, so the right unit is on the truck.

Signs your water heater should be replaced, not repaired

Leak from the tank body

Corrosion has breached the steel. No repair will hold, and the water damage grows every hour it runs.

Age past 10 to 12 years

Most tanks are built for roughly that span. A heater at that age with a problem is a good candidate for replacement.

Rust-colored hot water

Rust in hot water only, not cold, can mean the inside of the tank is corroding.

Second repair in a year

Parts are inexpensive, but repeated visits add up. A new unit with a warranty is often a better use of money.

Choosing the right replacement

Size matters more than most people think. A tank that is too small runs out in the middle of a shower, and one that is too large wastes energy keeping water hot. We size by the number of bathrooms, the number of people and your peak use, then check that the fuel line, vent and electrical supply can support it.

OptionBest forTrade-off
Standard gas tankMost homes with a gas line and a working flueLowest upfront cost, steady recovery
Standard electric tankHomes without gas, small spacesSimple install, higher energy cost in many homes
TanklessHouseholds that want endless hot water and save floor spaceHigher upfront cost, needs proper venting and gas capacity
Heat pump hybridHomes with space, airflow and an interest in lower operating costNeeds clearance and a condensate drain

If you are weighing on-demand heat, read our tankless water heater page before you decide. For a straight tank swap, the process below is typical.

1

Drain and disconnect

We shut off supply lines and gas or power, drain the tank and disconnect the old unit.

2

Set the new heater

We set the new tank on a drain pan or stand, connect water, gas or electric and vent, and add a new relief valve and discharge pipe.

3

Add what code needs

Expansion tanks, relief valve discharge piping and proper venting are checked or added.

4

Test and show you

We fire the heater, check for leaks, set the temperature and show you the shutoff valves.

Replacing a heater in a flood-prone basement

Calumet City's flood prevention page notes that the city participates in the National Flood Insurance Program and recommends protecting basements with measures like sewer backup valves. If your water heater sits in a basement that has taken on water before, ask us about raising the unit on a stand and routing its discharge to a floor drain protected from backflow.

Water that rises over the burner or electric connections damages a heater even if it never leaks. A stand a few inches high and a correctly placed drain pan cost little compared with replacing a unit twice. Our sump pump page covers what else keeps a basement dry.

What affects the cost of water heater replacement

Replacement cost factors

You hear a clear, written price before we remove the old heater. These are the pieces that make up the total.

Heater type and capacityGas, electric, tankless and hybrid units are priced very differently.
Venting and gas lineA new flue liner or a larger gas line adds time and materials.
Code upgradesExpansion tanks, relief valves, drain pans and shutoff valves may be needed.
Access and haul-awayStairs, tight closets and removal of the old tank affect the labor.
Hour of the callAfter-hours replacement costs more than a scheduled weekday install.

Tax credits and rebates

Some utilities and state or federal programs offer incentives for high-efficiency or heat pump water heaters. Programs change, so check current eligibility with your utility before you buy.
