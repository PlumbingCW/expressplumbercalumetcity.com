Under-Sink Tankless Water Heater (120V): Calumet City, IL Guide

A small electric unit under the sink can deliver hot water at one faucet without waiting for the tank. It has real limits. This guide explains them.

What a 120V under-sink unit does well

A 120V under-sink tankless heater, also called a point-of-use heater, heats water for a single faucet. It plugs into a standard outlet, and it helps when the main water heater is far from the sink and you wait a long time for hot water. It is not designed to run a shower or a whole kitchen.

The tradeoff is power. A 120V circuit supports a small element, so the unit raises the temperature of a trickle of water. In cold weather, with cold incoming water, the output may only be warm. It is best for hand washing and short kitchen tasks. For a whole-house solution, see our tankless water heater page.

What to check before buying

Outlet and circuit

The unit draws a significant current for a plug-in appliance. It needs a grounded outlet, preferably on its own circuit, and should be on a GFCI if local rules require it.

Space under the sink

Many units are about the size of a small cooler. Check the cabinet, the garbage disposal and the pipes.

Water pressure and flow

These units work with low flow. If the faucet is high-flow, water may not heat enough.

Hot and cold shutoffs

You will tie into the cold line. Check that the angle stop works and replace it if it is stiff.

If waiting for hot water is your main complaint, a recirculation pump may help more than an under-sink unit. It moves water through the pipes so hot water is nearby. A plumber can look at your layout and suggest the simplest fix.

How installation works

1

Pick the spot

The unit mounts under the sink or on the cabinet wall with room for the supply and the outlet.

2

Connect the water

A tee on the cold supply feeds the unit, and a short hose connects its output to the faucet.

3

Plug in and purge

Run water through the unit until air is out before the element turns on. Many units burn out if they run dry.

4

Set the temperature

Set it modestly. Hot water at a small faucet can scald.

Wiring a new outlet is electrician work. Do not use an extension cord, and keep the outlet away from where water can drip. If the work involves changing a supply line, the Illinois Department of Public Health says plumbing alterations must be done by a licensed plumber, so ask a professional if you are unsure.

Calumet City local note: the water entering the unit

The Calumet City Water Department's 2024 report says the city's water comes from Lake Michigan and that Chicago and Hammond supply it. The report we reviewed does not publish a hardness figure, so we cannot say how fast scale will build in a small heating element.

A cheap test strip tells you. If the water is hard, scale can shorten the life of a compact element. Ask about a small filter or softener, and look at our water softener and filtration page. The report also advises flushing taps and using certified filters if you are concerned about lead, and an under-sink space is where many people install them.

Cost factors

The unit itself is inexpensive compared with a whole-house heater. Installation depends on the outlet, the supply line and whether the sink has room. A plumber's visit for a small unit is usually short. Whole-house options cost much more and need venting or a larger electrical service.

What a 120V unit cannot do

A plug-in unit has a small heating element, so the flow is low and the temperature rise is limited. It is meant for a single sink, not a shower. In a cold winter the water may come out only warm at a normal flow, and slowing the tap down raises the temperature.

Do not expect it to supply a kitchen sink and a dishwasher at the same time. If you want one unit to serve more, a larger point-of-use or a whole-house system is the better fit.

Electrical and space checks

Check for a grounded outlet near the sink, ideally on a protected circuit, and for space inside the cabinet. Do not run an extension cord. A hardwired model must be installed by a qualified person, and the electrical part may need a separate trade. Ask your village whether a permit applies.
