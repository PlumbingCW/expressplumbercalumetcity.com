Planning a water heater installation

An installation is different from an emergency replacement because you control the timing. That gives you time to choose the type, check your venting and gas capacity, and decide whether to upgrade. If your tank has already failed, our emergency water heater replacement page covers the faster path.

Our plumbers are on the line 24/7, but installs are scheduled visits. We usually need a short call or a photo of the data plate on your current heater, and a look at the venting, gas line or breaker panel.

Sizing: what the numbers mean

Tank capacity

A 40 gallon tank suits many small households. A larger family with several bathrooms may need 50 or 75 gallons.

First-hour rating

This tells you how much hot water the heater can deliver in a busy hour. It matters more than gallons stored.

Flow rate for tankless

Add up the fixtures you run at once. If the shower, dishwasher and washer run together, you need a unit that can keep up.

Recovery and efficiency

Gas recovers faster than electric. A higher efficiency heater costs more up front and less to run.

What a proper installation includes

1

Prep and removal

We shut off the supply, drain the old unit, disconnect and haul it away.

2

Set and connect

We place the new heater on a stand or pan, connect water lines, gas or electric and vent.

3

Safety devices

We install a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe, add an expansion tank where the system needs one and check for proper venting.

4

Start up and walk through

We light or power the heater, check for leaks, set the temperature and show you the shutoffs.

Installing in a Calumet City basement

Many homes in the south suburbs keep the water heater in a basement, and Calumet City's flood prevention page recommends several measures for basements that take on water, including sewer backup valves and protecting openings. If your basement has flooded, ask us about raising the heater on a stand and moving it away from a floor drain.

Lake Michigan supplies the city's water, as the 2024 city report states, and the incoming water is cold in winter. A larger or better-insulated tank can help you recover from a cold-weather morning rush, and our plumbers can recommend a size for your household.
