What plumbing installation covers

Plumbing installation is anything new or relocated: a basement bathroom, a kitchen remodel, a laundry hookup, a new shower or a utility sink in the garage. It is planned work, so we start with a conversation about what you want and what the existing pipes can support.

For emergencies, such as a burst line or failed heater, we have separate pages. If a fixture has failed, see our faucet repair or toilet repair and installation pages. This page is for the planned work.

Common installation projects

Basement bathroom

This needs supply lines, a drain that works with gravity or an ejector pump and a vent. Below-grade drainage is the main design question.

Kitchen remodel

New sink, dishwasher, disposal and ice maker hookups, often with relocated lines.

Laundry room

Supply valves, a standpipe with a trap and a drain pan if the room is above living space.

Shower and tub replacement

New valves, drains and sometimes a larger drain line.

Water filtration and softener lines

Bypass valves, pressure gauges and drain connections.

Outdoor spigots and utility sinks

Frost-proof hose bibs and a laundry sink for the garage or basement.

Remodeling a Calumet City basement

The city's flood prevention page recommends measures for basements that take on water, including floor drain plugs, standpipes, overhead sewers and sewer backup valves, and it notes that sump pump and sewer backup coverage are insurance riders that cost extra. If you are finishing a basement, ask us about where the fixtures sit relative to the sewer and what protects them.

A basement bathroom is a good time to check that your sump discharge goes outdoors and that your cleanout is accessible. It is much cheaper to add a valve or a cleanout before the walls close than after.

Gas piping and gas smells: safety first

If you smell gas, leave the building right away, do not use switches, lighters or phones inside, and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Do this before you call a plumber. We do not promise a response time for a gas leak, because the utility and the fire department are the right first call.

Planned gas work is different. Adding a gas line for a range, dryer, water heater or boiler is piping work that needs a pressure test and, in most villages, a permit and an inspection. Ask who holds the license for gas piping and what the permit covers before you agree to a price.
