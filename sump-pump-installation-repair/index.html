Why sump pumps fail

Stuck float switch

The float jams against the pit wall or debris. The pump never turns on.

Power loss

Storms often knock out power right when the pump is needed. A battery backup keeps it running.

Worn motor

Pumps wear out. A pump that runs constantly or short cycles is nearing the end.

Clogged intake or discharge

Sediment, gravel or a frozen discharge line stops flow.

Failed check valve

Without one, water drains back into the pit and the pump cycles forever.

Installing a new sump pump system

1

Check the pit and drain tile

We check whether the pit is deep enough and whether water enters from drain tile or from the floor.

2

Set the pump

We place a submersible pump on a stable base, set the float so it does not rub the wall and add a check valve.

3

Route the discharge

We run the discharge pipe outdoors, away from the foundation, with a way to avoid freezing.

4

Test and add backup

We fill the pit to test the pump and install a battery backup if you want one.

Flood risk in Calumet City

An ABC7 analysis of First Street Foundation data, reported in 2023, found that nearly half of Calumet City homes face at least a 1 in 4 chance of flooding over 30 years. The city's flood prevention page lists a levee on the Little Calumet River built after the 1981 and 1982 floods and notes six major river floods since 1981.

Whether water comes from the river, the sewer or the ground, a sump pump with a battery backup and a working discharge is one of the more practical protections. The city page also notes that sump pump failure coverage is an insurance rider and costs extra, so ask your agent before the next storm.
