What a sewer camera inspection is

A sewer camera inspection puts a waterproof camera on a flexible rod and feeds it through a cleanout or the toilet flange into your main drain. The plumber watches a live video as the camera moves toward the street. We note the pipe material, joints, cracks, roots, offsets and low spots, and we mark their distance.

You see what we see. The footage can be saved so you can share it with a contractor, an insurance adjuster or a home buyer. A locator in the camera head lets us mark a defect on the ground above, so a repair crew knows exactly where to dig.

When an inspection makes sense

Before buying a house

A seller's inspection report may not include the sewer line. A camera can reveal root intrusion, bellies and collapsed sections before closing.

After repeat clogs

If the same drain keeps backing up, the cause may be a broken line a cable cannot fix.

Before a major repair

If you are about to dig or reline, a camera confirms the scope, and that saves money.

When you see sewage smells or wet spots

Odors, a soggy patch of lawn and sinkholes are signs worth checking.

How an inspection works, step by step

1

Find access

We use a cleanout when possible. If there is none, we may pull a toilet to feed the camera.

2

Run the camera

We feed the camera toward the street and watch the monitor for defects.

3

Mark and measure

We note distance and depth, and mark surface points above defects.

4

Explain the findings

We show you the footage and tell you which defects are urgent, which can wait and which are normal for pipe of that age.

A camera inspection is not a repair, and it does not guarantee a line will never fail. It shows the condition on the day of the inspection. If the footage shows a defect, our sewer line repair team can explain the options with the same video in front of us.

Buying or selling in Calumet City and Lansing

Real estate in the south suburbs often involves older properties, and a 2025 water affordability plan for Lansing, published through the University of Illinois Chicago, reports that 39 percent of homes in that village were built in 1959 or earlier. In a house that old, the sewer lateral may be original, which is a good reason to put a camera on it before closing.

If you are selling, a camera inspection ahead of listing can head off a buyer's surprise and give you time to repair on your own schedule. If you are buying, ask for the footage and the plumber's marks, and bring them to the negotiation. See our Lansing, IL page for what we cover there.

Is an inspection covered by insurance?

Usually not as a routine check. Some policies cover inspection tied to a covered loss. If you need the footage for a claim, tell us when you book.
