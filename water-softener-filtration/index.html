Start with what is in your water

A water softener, a carbon filter and a reverse osmosis system do different things. A softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium and prevents scale. A carbon filter reduces chlorine taste and odor. Reverse osmosis reduces a wider range of dissolved substances. The right choice depends on what you want to remove.

That is why we start with a test. A hardness strip costs a few dollars. A lab test for lead or other contaminants costs more and tells you more. We do not recommend a system until we know what problem it is meant to solve.

What Calumet City's water report says

The Calumet City Water Department's 2024 report says the city draws 100 percent of its drinking water from Lake Michigan, with 20 percent supplied by Chicago and 80 percent by Hammond. It says the water met all U.S. EPA and state drinking water standards, and it notes fluoride is added and that sodium has no state or federal limit.

The report also lists a lead 90th percentile of 17 parts per billion against a 15 ppb action level. It says lead comes primarily from service lines and home plumbing. It advises flushing taps and using a filter certified by an accredited body if you are concerned. The report we reviewed does not list a hardness figure, so we do not quote one. Test your tap, or ask the city water department.

Hardness: test, do not guess

Because the city report we reviewed does not publish hardness, we recommend a test strip or a call to the Calumet City Water Department at the number on its report. Hardness can vary with the supplier, and the city's water comes from both Hammond and Chicago.

If your test shows low hardness, a softener may be unnecessary. If it shows high hardness, a softener protects your water heater and fixtures. Either way, the sodium in a softener is a consideration for people on a sodium-restricted diet, and the city's report says to consult a physician.
