Slow Draining Tub but Not Clogged? A Calumet City, IL Guide

Why a tub drains slowly after the stopper is clean

A tub that drains slowly with a clean stopper usually has a partial blockage further down, a venting problem or a pipe that does not slope properly. Hair and soap form a film inside the trap and the branch line, and that film narrows the pipe without ever making a complete clog.

Start with the simple checks below. If they do not help, the next step is a cable or a camera, and our drain cleaning page explains what that visit looks like.

Checks you can do yourself

1

Remove and clean the stopper and strainer

Lift out the stopper or unscrew the strainer and clear hair from underneath.

2

Check the overflow

The overflow plate on the front of the tub connects to the drain, and it can hold a hair mat.

3

Try a plastic drain tool

A thin barbed plastic strip pulls hair from the first few inches of the drain.

4

Flush with hot water

After clearing, run hot water for a few minutes. Avoid boiling water on plastic pipes.

If the tub still drains slowly, test other fixtures. A slow tub plus a gurgling sink or a slow toilet suggests the problem is shared.

The vent: the hidden part of the drain

Every drain needs air. A vent pipe, usually going through the roof, lets air in behind the water so it flows smoothly. A blocked vent acts like a thumb over a drinking straw, and the water slows and gurgles. Leaves, a bird's nest or ice can block a roof vent. A plumber can check it, and an inspection with a camera can find a sag in the drain.

Gurgling from the tub

Air is being pulled through the trap because the vent is restricted.

Sewer smell in the bathroom

A dried-out trap or a vent problem can let gas into the room.

Fixtures affect each other

The toilet flush makes the tub bubble. The vent or branch is shared.

If the cause is deeper, a sewer camera inspection shows the pipe and where it dips. For repeat slow drains, hydro jetting cleans soap film from the pipe wall.

Calumet City local note: the plumbing code and your drain

The Illinois Department of Public Health says the Illinois Plumbing Code, found in Title 77 of the Illinois Administrative Code, is the minimum standard for plumbing practices in the state, and that licensed plumbers must install, alter and repair plumbing. Drain slope and venting are code matters, which is why a poorly vented or flat drain run is a real defect and not just an annoyance.

If you finished a basement bathroom or an older remodel and the tub there drains slowly, ask what permit and inspection records exist for the work. Permit rules vary, so ask the Calumet City Building and Zoning Department. A plumber can also check whether a tub in the basement is draining by gravity or through a pump.

What to do before you call

Try these first

  • ✓Remove the stopper and clear hair from the drain with a plastic drain tool or your fingers
  • ✓Run the sink and toilet nearby and listen for gurgling, which points to a vent or a shared drain
  • ✓Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a tub that holds standing water, because it can splash and burn
  • ✓Call if more than one fixture drains slowly or water comes up in a floor drain

What affects the cost

A cable visit for a single branch is a short job. A camera inspection, a vent repair or a drain section replacement costs more and takes longer. A written quote after the plumber has seen the problem lists what is included, and after-hours visits cost more than daytime appointments.
