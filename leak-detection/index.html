A leak you cannot see is still an emergency

Water through a ceiling, wet drywall that keeps coming back or the sound of running water behind a wall all call for emergency leak detection. Our plumbers are on the line 24/7, and a same-day visit is common. Shut off the main valve if water is actively spreading, then call.

Leaks that run quietly are the expensive ones. A pinhole in a copper line inside a wall can feed mold for weeks, and a slow leak under a slab can wash soil from beneath it. Finding the exact point early is almost always cheaper than guessing and cutting.

Signs of a hidden leak

Water meter moves with taps off

Close every fixture and look at the meter's leak indicator or dial. If it keeps moving, water is leaving the system somewhere.

Warm spot on the floor

A hot line leaking under a slab can warm the floor above it. A single warm patch in a cool room is worth a call.

Stains, bubbling paint or musty smell

Ceiling stains, peeling wallpaper and a damp smell without an obvious source point to a supply or drain line behind the surface.

Unexplained bill increase

A jump in usage with the same household habits suggests a leak in a toilet, an irrigation line or buried service pipe.

How we find the leak without tearing the house apart

We start by isolating the system. Closing individual fixture valves and watching the meter tells us whether the leak is on the supply side, in a fixture or in a drain. That step often saves an hour of searching.

Then we use tools suited to the surface. Acoustic sensors hear the sound of a pressurized leak through floors and walls. Thermal imaging shows moisture and temperature differences behind drywall. A moisture meter confirms the edges of a wet area. Pipe locators trace buried lines, and tracer gas helps when the sound is too faint to pinpoint.

Slab leaks: what to expect

A slab leak is a leak in a line that runs beneath or inside a concrete floor. The signs are a warm spot, the sound of running water, wet flooring or a high bill. Once we pinpoint the line, you have three common options: reroute the line through walls or attic, repair it in the slab, or repipe the affected section. We lay out the trade-offs and cost factors for each so you decide.

When the leak is in a drain line, we may recommend a sewer camera inspection to see the pipe. If a supply line has failed more than once, a repiping plan can replace the weak section instead of patching it again.

Why Calumet City water leaks are worth catching early

The city's 2024 water quality report says Calumet City draws all its drinking water from Lake Michigan. It lists a lead 90th percentile of 17 parts per billion against a 15 ppb action level, and notes that lead in drinking water comes primarily from service lines and home plumbing. If you are opening walls or lines anyway, that is a sensible moment to ask what your supply pipe is made of.

The report also points residents to the city's lead service line inventory on calumetcity.org. A leak on the service line is often your chance to replace the damaged section with modern pipe, and our water service line repair page covers what that involves.

Ceiling and wall leaks

A stain on a ceiling or a damp wall tells you where water arrived, not where it started. Water runs along pipes and joists before it shows, so the source can be a bathroom above, a supply line in the wall or a roof problem that only looks like plumbing.

Shut off the water to the fixture above if you can, and put a bucket under any drip. Do not poke a hole in a bulging ceiling without a bucket and a plan, because it can release a lot of water at once. Take photos before anything is opened.

Basements, crawl spaces and leaks under the floor

Many homes in the area have basements, and some have crawl spaces or slab floors. A basement lets us see most of the supply and drain piping from below. A crawl space or slab hides it, which is where slab leak methods come in.

Signs under the floor include a warm spot on a slab, the sound of running water when everything is off, or a musty smell that returns after cleaning. Tell us which type of foundation you have when you call so the right equipment comes.

Sprinkler and irrigation line leaks

Outdoor lines can leak long before a puddle shows. A lawn that stays soggy near a head, a pressure drop at the spigot or a meter that runs with the house shut off can all point outside. Close the valve feeding the system and see whether the meter stops. If it does, the leak is in the irrigation line, and we can help find it.

From finding the leak to repairing it

Locating a leak and fixing it are two steps, and you can have them done by the same plumber or separately. We tell you what we found, where it is and what a repair would involve before work starts. If you only want the location marked so you can decide, say so when you call.

What to do while you wait

Before the plumber arrives

  • ✓Turn off every tap and appliance that uses water, then check whether the meter still moves
  • ✓If it moves, shut the water off at the main valve and leave it off until we talk
  • ✓Move electronics and stored items away from wet floors, and stay away from outlets near standing water
  • ✓Take photos of wet spots, stains and the meter reading for your records and your insurer
  • ✓Do not cut into drywall or a slab yourself, because the wet spot is often not where the leak is
