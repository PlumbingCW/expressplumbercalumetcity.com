Sump pump prices vary because every basement is different. This guide explains what drives the number so you can compare quotes fairly.

What decides the price of a sump pump install

A sump pump installation price depends on whether you already have a pit, the size and type of pump, whether you add a battery backup, how far the discharge line must run and what is under the floor. A swap into an existing pit is a short job. A new pit cut into a concrete slab is a bigger one.

We do not publish a flat price because the range is wide and depends on the house. A written quote after a visit lists each item. For repair and replacement details, see our sump pump page.

Submersible or pedestal?

Submersible

Sits in the pit, runs quieter and handles heavier use. It is the usual choice for most basements.

Pedestal

The motor sits above the pit on a stand. It can be cheaper and easier to service, but louder and less suited to tight pits.

Primary plus backup

Two pumps, one on a battery, keep the basement dry when storms knock out power. They are practical where basements flood.

Water-powered backup

Uses municipal water pressure to run a backup. It depends on local rules and costs more.

Where the discharge should go

A pump moves water out of the pit, but the discharge pipe decides whether the water comes back. Run it outdoors, well away from the foundation, and not into the sanitary sewer. Keep it from freezing with a proper slope and an outlet that cannot be blocked by snow. Rules about where discharge may go vary, so ask your local building department.

If water keeps returning because the grade slopes toward the house, a pump alone will cycle all day. Our basement waterproofing page covers other ways to keep water moving away.

Calumet City local note: flood risk, insurance riders and the city's advice

An ABC7 analysis of First Street Foundation data, reported in 2023, found that nearly half of Calumet City homes have at least a 1 in 4 chance of flooding over 30 years. The city's flood prevention page says sump pump and sewer backup coverage are riders that cost extra and vary by insurer, so the question to ask your agent is whether your policy includes them.

The same page lists the Little Calumet River levee and the city's participation in the National Flood Insurance Program and Community Rating System. Whatever you spend on a pump, pair it with a battery backup and ask about coverage. See our Lansing page if you live just south of Calumet City, where the levee protects both communities.

How to compare quotes

Ask each plumber to list

  • ✓Pump brand, model and horsepower
  • ✓Whether a new pit, check valve and discharge line are included
  • ✓Backup type and expected run time
  • ✓Where the discharge ends and how it avoids freezing
  • ✓Warranty on the pump and on the labor

What a typical install includes

A basic replacement swaps the pump, check valve and a short length of discharge pipe in an existing pit. A new install adds more work: cutting the slab, digging the pit, setting a liner, running the discharge line to the outside and sealing the lid. The second job takes longer and costs more because of the concrete work.

Ask whether the quote includes the check valve, the pit liner, the discharge pipe and any permit your village requires. A line item you do not see is often an extra later.

Backup power and alarms

A pump that fails during a storm is usually one that lost power. A battery backup pump or a water-powered backup adds cost, but it is the part that keeps working when the outage hits. A high-water alarm is cheap and tells you the pit is filling faster than the pump can empty it. Ask for both options in the same quote so you can compare.

How to compare two quotes

Put the quotes side by side and check the pump size, the pit, the discharge route and the warranty terms. A lower price with a smaller pump or no check valve is not the same job. Ask what is covered if the pump fails in the first year and who you call when it does.
