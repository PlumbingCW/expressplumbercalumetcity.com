When a water heater problem is an emergency

A leaking tank, a gas smell near the heater or water that is scalding hot at the tap is an emergency, and our plumbers answer 24/7, including winter nights when a cold house makes it worse. We offer same-day visits and will tell you on the call whether anything needs to be shut off first.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us. If the tank is leaking, close the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the heater, turn the gas control to pilot or the electric breaker off, and keep the floor around it clear.

What is wrong with the heater? Common symptoms

No hot water at all

On a gas heater the cause is often the pilot, thermocouple or gas control. On an electric heater it is usually a failed element, a tripped high-limit switch or a bad thermostat.

Lukewarm water that runs out fast

A broken dip tube, a burned-out lower element or heavy sediment can cut the usable hot water in half.

Rumbling or popping from the tank

Sediment at the bottom traps steam bubbles. Flushing may help, but a tank with years of buildup may be near the end of its life.

Water around the base

A leak at a fitting or relief valve is repairable. A leak through the tank itself means the tank has failed.

Rusty or smelly hot water

A depleted anode rod can let the tank corrode, and bacteria can cause a rotten-egg smell. Replacing the anode rod extends the life of a sound tank.

A leak through the tank cannot be patched. In that case our water heater replacement team can often set a new unit the same day, and if you are curious about on-demand heat, our tankless water heater page covers the trade-offs.

Gas, electric and hybrid heaters

Gas heaters add venting and combustion to the repair picture. We check the flue draft, the burner and the gas control, and we do not leave a gas heater running if the venting looks unsafe. Electric heaters are simpler, but heating elements and thermostats still fail, and a failed element can be replaced without a new tank.

Hybrid heat pump water heaters sit in between. They have a compressor, a fan and a condensate line, and they need airflow and space. If you have one, tell us the make and model when you call so we bring the right parts.

Hard water, sediment and the Lake Michigan supply

Calumet City's 2024 water quality report says the city draws its water from Lake Michigan and does not list a hardness figure in the report we reviewed. Hardness varies by supply and by home, so we recommend a simple test strip or a call to the city water department before assuming you need a softener.

Whatever the water, a tank heater benefits from an annual drain of a few gallons to carry out sediment, and the anode rod should be checked every few years. Those two steps are the most common reason a sound tank lasts longer. Our water softener and filtration page explains what testing tells you.

A leaking water heater: where the water is coming from

Water around a heater is not always the tank. Leaks often come from a loose supply fitting, the drain valve, or the temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe. Those are repairs. Water that seeps from the tank body itself means the steel has rusted through, and that heater needs to be replaced.

Dry the area and watch for a few minutes. A drip that follows the pipe down to a fitting is usually fixable. Water that appears from under the jacket or from the bottom seam is not. Shut off the cold water supply to the heater, turn off the gas or power, and call. Do not plug a relief valve to stop it dripping.

Warranty and insurance

Check your manufacturer warranty. Some parts, like elements and anode rods, carry coverage even on older heaters. Homeowner insurance may cover a sudden tank rupture but typically excludes gradual leakage.
