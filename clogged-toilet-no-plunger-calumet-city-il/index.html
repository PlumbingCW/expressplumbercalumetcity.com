Clogged Toilet and No Plunger? A Calumet City, IL Guide

It is late, the toilet is clogged and the plunger is missing. These household fixes often work, and a few common ideas do more harm than good.

First: stop the water

If the bowl is rising, do not flush again. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise. That stops the tank from refilling the bowl. If the water is near the rim, put on gloves and take out a few cups with a bucket to leave room.

Then try one of the methods below. If nothing works, or if other drains are slow, the clog may be in the main line, and our drain cleaning team can help day or night.

Household fixes that often work

1

Hot water and dish soap

Squirt a good amount of dish soap into the bowl and pour in a bucket of hot, not boiling, water from waist height. Wait 15 minutes.

2

A bottle method

A firm, flexible plastic bottle with the cap on can act like a small plunger. Push it into the drain opening and squeeze quickly in short strokes.

3

A wire hanger

Straighten a coat hanger, wrap the end in a rag and gently work it into the drain. Be careful not to scratch the porcelain.

4

Wait and retry

Soft clogs sometimes soften on their own. Try again after an hour or after the soap has worked.

Use the same rule for all of them: gentle, patient and no chemicals. If a hanger snags, stop.

Things not to do

Flush wipes and paper towelsThey do not break down and cause main line clogs

When a clog is more than the toilet

Other fixtures are slow

A tub or sink that drains slowly while you clear the toilet points to the main line.

Gurgling or bubbling

Air pushed through other traps means the line is restricted.

A floor drain backs up

The lowest drain shows the main line problem first.

It keeps returning

A recurring clog may mean a tilted toilet, a partial blockage in the line or roots.

In those cases, a cable or a camera is the next step. Our sewer line cleaning page explains how we clear a main line, and it also covers when to use a camera.

Calumet City local note: backups, storms and the city sewer

The city of Calumet City's flood prevention page suggests floor drain plugs, standpipes, overhead sewers and sewer backup valves for homes that see repeat backups. If a toilet and the basement floor drain back up together during or after heavy rain, the cause may be the public sewer, and no plunger will fix it.

Local news coverage of the July 2023 flooding quoted officials who said much of the damage came from sewer backups, and reported that one resident's insurance policy excluded drain, toilet, shower and sink backups. If your toilet backs up during a storm, document it and call your insurer as well as a plumber.

Prevent the next one

Flush only toilet paper and human waste. Keep wipes, paper towels, feminine products and cotton swabs out, even if the package says flushable. Buy a plunger with a flange, a rubber extension that fits into the drain, since that is the type that works on a toilet. Keep it near the toilet.

What to do after the water goes down

Flush once and watch the bowl fill. If it rises quickly toward the rim, stop and wait. Do not flush again until the level drops. A second flush on a partial clog is how most overflows happen.

Once the toilet works, run the sink and the tub nearby. If those gurgle or drain slowly, the clog may be in the branch line and not the toilet. That is a drain cleaning job, and a plunger will not reach it.

Keeping it from happening again

Most toilet clogs come from too much paper, wipes labeled flushable or a toy that fell in. Keep a plunger in the bathroom and tell everyone in the house what belongs in the bowl. If one toilet clogs often while the others do not, ask a plumber to check the fixture, because a worn flapper or a partial blockage in the trap can be the cause.

If the household fixes fail at night, our emergency line covers Calumet City and Lansing. See our service areas page for the other nearby towns.

Fixture and drain work in Illinois is covered by the Illinois Plumbing Code, which is why a toilet that keeps backing up after a plunger or a closet auger should go to a licensed plumber and not to a stronger chemical.
