A water service line break is an emergency

When the service line between the street and your house breaks, water pours into the soil or your basement and pressure drops in the house. Our plumbers take these calls 24/7, including nights and holidays, and we can often start the same day. Shut off the main valve inside the house if you can reach it.

If water is surfacing in the yard or the street near the meter, that may be the city side of the line. Call the city water department for the main and call us for the line to the house. If you cannot tell, call us and we will help sort it out.

Signs of a leaking or failing service line

Pressure drops in the whole house

A leak before the house means less water reaches every fixture.

A wet or soft spot between meter and house

Water saturates the soil above the line and shows at the surface.

Meter spins with everything off

That means water is leaving after the meter. See our water meter page for the test.

Dirty or discolored water

A break can let soil into the line, and a plumber should check it.

Lead service lines: what the city says

The city's 2024 water quality report says Calumet City is responsible for providing high quality drinking water and removing lead pipes, but it cannot control the materials in your home plumbing. The report lists a lead 90th percentile of 17 ppb against an action level of 15 ppb, and it says the city's lead service line inventory is posted on calumetcity.org.

If your line is old, look at the pipe where it enters the house. Lead is dull gray and soft enough to scratch with a key, and a magnet will not stick. If you are unsure, send a photo to the city inventory contact or call us. When we replace a service line, we can tell you what material came out and what went in. See our repiping page if the pipes inside the house are also due.

Who owns which part of the line

In most communities the city owns the main in the street and the homeowner owns the pipe from the curb or meter to the house. Ask Calumet City's water department where responsibility starts for your address, and whether the city offers programs for replacement.

That answer changes who pays and who digs. If the leak is on the city side, the water department fixes it. If it is on your side, we do, and we can help you document the location for any program that applies.

Underground and buried line leaks

A buried supply line can leak for a long time before it shows. Signs include a water bill that climbs with no change in habits, a soft or green patch in the yard, a meter that moves with everything off, or low pressure in the house.

Do the meter test first: turn off every tap and appliance and watch whether the dial still turns. If it does and the leak is not inside, the line between the meter and the house is the next suspect. Call before digging, and ask your village or the city water department who locates buried utilities before an excavation starts.

Low water pressure

Low pressure at every tap points to the supply side. Causes include a partly closed main valve, a failing pressure regulator, corroded pipe that has narrowed inside or a leaking service line. If only one fixture is weak, the cause is local, such as a clogged aerator or cartridge. Tell us whether the whole house or one fixture is affected.
