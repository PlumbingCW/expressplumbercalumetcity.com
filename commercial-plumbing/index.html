Plumbing emergencies at a business

A restroom that will not drain, a kitchen line that has backed up or a burst pipe in a ceiling can close a business for the day. We take commercial calls 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and we can often come the same day. Shut off the water to the affected area if you can, and call.

If the situation is unsafe, such as sewage on a customer floor, a gas smell or water near electrical equipment, close the area, tell staff to keep clear and call us. For a gas smell, leave and call the gas utility or 911 first.

What we handle for commercial properties

Restaurant and kitchen lines

Grease interceptors, floor drains, three-compartment sinks and dishwashers back up when grease builds. We clear lines and talk through maintenance schedules.

Retail and office restrooms

Clogged toilets, slow urinals, leaking flush valves and running tanks drive up water bills and annoy customers.

Water heaters and hot water

A failed tank or a heater that cannot keep up affects food service, salons and gyms.

Leaks and breaks

Ceiling leaks, burst supply lines and slab leaks in a shared building affect multiple tenants.

Backflow and water service

Commercial buildings often have backflow assemblies that need annual testing under local rules.

Property manager support

We document work for managers and owners, and photograph before and after for your records.

Many commercial blockages are grease-related, and a regular hydro jetting visit costs less than an emergency call. For a persistent drain problem, a camera inspection identifies the cause. If the problem is a failed water heater, see our water heater replacement page.

Businesses along 159th Street, Sibley Boulevard and River Oaks

Calumet City's commercial strips include the 159th Street corridor, Sibley Boulevard, Wentworth Avenue and the River Oaks area. Older retail buildings can have tenant spaces that share a stack, and a clog in one unit can back up into the next.

When we are called to a multi-tenant building, we ask whether neighboring spaces have symptoms, and we locate cleanouts before we start. That saves time and tells you whether the problem is in your unit or in the shared line. If you manage the building, ask us about a maintenance visit.

Insurance and landlord responsibility

Check your lease to see who handles plumbing inside a unit and in shared lines. Business property policies may cover sudden water damage but not gradual leaks. Keep our invoice and photos for your claim.
